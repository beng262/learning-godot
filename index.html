How this book works

Every chapter teaches one thing about Godot, shows it working, and then hands it to you. The code runs right here: press Run on any example, change it, and it runs again as you type.

Each chapter has three kinds of doing. A live tutorial builds one thing with you, a step at a time, in a single editor: your code runs while you type, each step's checks tick as you meet them, and the next step opens when they all pass. Then exercises leave you on your own, with Check, hints and a solution when you want one. And the workshop is a small working copy of the Godot editor, for the parts of Godot that are clicked rather than typed.

Nothing needs installing to read it or to do any of that. GDScript runs in this page on a small interpreter written for the book – the language as Godot 4 has it, so 5 / 2 is 2, a lambda captures by value and await waits. The parts of Godot that cannot live in a web page – the renderer, physics, the real editor – are explained and shown.

Alongside the engine runs a real game, Little Hours: a cosy life game with 50 rooms, a town of 15 people and an on-device AI for small talk. Every chapter ends with where its idea lives in that game. Little Hours from A to Z takes the game one rule at a time – the clock, the farm, the neighbours, the phone, saving – and has you rebuild each in miniature. Inside the code walks every one of its files. You do not need the project: the code is quoted, explained and linked.

Your progress stays with you

Finished tutorials and solved exercises are ticked and your code is kept – in this browser only. Nothing is sent anywhere.
