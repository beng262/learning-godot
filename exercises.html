Part three · Chapter nine

Your turn

Sixteen exercises, in order. The first three change data and cannot break anything. Then content, then behaviour and graphics. The last five are the real work: two of them are bugs that actually shipped in this game, and one is a refactor.

Before you start

You need the project open and Godot 4.7. Run ./tools-test.sh once from the godot/ folder to check everything is green before you change anything — a baseline you trust is worth the forty seconds.

Part seven

Sixteen exercises

In order. The first three change data and cannot break anything; the middle three add real content; then behaviour and graphics, and the last five are the real work: two debugging exercises drawn from bugs that actually shipped, and one refactor. Every one ends with something you can run to know whether you got it right.

Warm-up· ten minutes

1. Change what a piece of furniture says

scripts/RoomData.gd

Find the couch in "living" and rewrite its flavor string — the line shown when you tap something that has no action. Then find a piece with an action and notice the difference: flavour is what a thing says, action is what it does.

Verify Run the game, walk to it, press E. Nothing else needs to change: nothing else in the codebase knows that string exists.
Warm-up· ten minutes

2. Break a test on purpose

scripts/Player.gd → SPEED

Change SPEED from 280 to 150 and run the suite. The walking group asserts that a walk gets above 240px/s within half a second, so it should go red — and tell you the number it actually saw. Put it back to 280 and watch it go green. This is the fastest way to learn what the suite is actually measuring.

Verify ./tools-test.sh — expect walking gets up to speed within half a second — 150px/s of a possible 280.
Warm-up· fifteen minutes

3. Make something overhead

scripts/Assets.gd → OVERHEAD

Pick a tall piece — topiary, or one of the allotment hedges — and add it with a fraction, say 0.5. Walk behind it and then in front of it. Then try 0.9 and see it go wrong: nearly the whole hedge draws over your head and you walk through it like fog. The right number is the one where the split is at the height of a shoulder.

Verify Your eyes, then ./tools-test.sh — the depth group counts the overhead slices and will notice if the piece stopped splitting.
Content· thirty minutes

4. Put a new piece of furniture in a room

scripts/Assets.gd → ART · scripts/RoomData.gd

Choose a PNG from assets/decor/, give it an id in ART at scale 2, then add one _o(...) row to a room. The rect is the collision footprint, so make it roughly the size of the art at the base — the sprite draws at its own size regardless. Give it a flavor so tapping it says something.

Verify ./tools-test.sh catches a missing file or a missing id before you ever launch. Then run the game and look at the shadow: if it sits wrong, the art has padding and you have just met base_offset().
Content· an hour

5. Add a room to the town

scripts/RoomData.gd (six tables) · scripts/Assets.gd

A room needs an entry in ROOM_IDS (which is also the walking order — where you put it decides which doors it sits between), ROOM_NAMES, MAP_SHORT, MAP_ICONS, MAP_CELLS, ROOM_MULT, and a list of furniture. Deliberately forget one of them first.

Verify ./tools-test.sh will name the table you forgot — that is what those 668 data checks are for. The plan grid check will also catch it if your new room sits on another room's square.
Content· an hour

6. Add a neighbour

scripts/NeighborData.gd · optionally scripts/LoveData.gd

Five beats, in the order they should come out, one a day. Give them a room that exists and an icon that exists. If you also add them to LoveData.CANDIDATES they become romanceable, which needs four flirt lines — one for each way the player can carry themselves — and a warms_to / wary_of pair.

Verify The data group checks they live somewhere real, have something to say, and have a line for every manner. A missing flirt line is caught by name.
Behaviour· an hour

7. Add a new interaction

scripts/RoomData.gd · scripts/Overlays.gd

Give a piece of furniture an action string nothing else uses — say "radio". Nothing else is needed to route it: Main._run() falls through to _overlays.open(action, ctx) for anything it does not recognise. Then add "radio": _build_radio() to the match in Overlays.open() and write the builder. A builder only appends rows to _body; the card, scroll, sizing and pop-in are already done.

Verify Add "radio" to CARD_KINDS in the suite. It will then be opened and measured on every run, and a card that renders empty will fail rather than looking fine.
Behaviour· two hours

8. Write a check of your own

tests/Suite.gd

Here is one worth having, which does not exist yet: every walk-to anchor should be standable. Each piece with an act point sends the resident there before the action fires — so assert, for every room and every piece, that its act is inside Game.BOUNDS and that Player._blocked() is false there. An anchor inside a wall means an interaction that only ever fires on the stall timer.

Verify Break it on purpose: move one anchor into a wall and confirm the check names that exact piece. Then decide whether the failures it finds are bugs or bad anchors — that judgement is the actual exercise.
Behaviour· an afternoon

9. Make the grocer seasonal

scripts/Game.gd → GROCER_STOCK · scripts/Overlays.gd → _build_grocer()

Right now the grocer sells the same four things forever. Make the stock depend on Game.season_index(): strawberries in summer, pumpkins in autumn, and something dull in winter at a higher price. The interesting part is not the code, it is keeping the invariant — every price must stay above what the market pays, or the player can buy and re-sell at a profit.

Verify The existing price check only looks at the flat list. Extend it to walk all four seasons, so the invariant is proved for every one of them rather than for whichever happens to be loaded.
Graphics· thirty minutes

10. Light something

scripts/Assets.gd → LIGHTS

Add a light to a piece that has none — the bakery's bread shelf, the harbour's brazier of a catch box, the gelato freezer. Then get the lift wrong on purpose: set it to 0 and see the light pool on the floor like a puddle instead of glowing from the object. Then set flicker on a lamp and notice that it now reads as a fault rather than a flame.

Verify Set the clock to night to see it — Game.clock_t = Game.DAY_LENGTH — or wait eight minutes of play. The suite does not check colour, and could not: this one is your eyes.
Graphics· an afternoon

11. Replace the hover highlight with a shader

scripts/RoomView.gd → set_hover() · a new shaders/outline.gdshader

Pointing at furniture currently brightens the whole sprite with modulate and lifts it two pixels. Write the outline shader from Part five, put it on the hovered sprite, and take the modulate away — keep the lift, it is doing real work. The interesting parts are the two traps: a sprite whose art touches its own texture edge has nowhere to draw the line, and a ShaderMaterial shared between sprites will outline all of them at once unless you duplicate() it.

Verify Hover one piece and confirm exactly one piece outlines. Then run ./tools-test.sh: the depth group will tell you immediately if adding a material disturbed any sprite's position or z, which is the kind of thing that is invisible until it is not.
Debugging· forty minutes

12. Find the shadow bug yourself

scripts/Avatar.gd → _measure_shadow()

Replace the call to _foot_span() with the old version — _shadow_meas_w = float(used.size.x) * sc * 0.9 — and run the game. Look at the character. The shadow is now sized from the bounding box of the whole sprite, and this character's arms reach both edges of their frame, so it comes out half again wider than they stand.

The lesson is not the fix, it is the shape of the mistake: a bounding box tells you how much room the art needs, never where it touches the floor. Every engine gives you the first and none of them give you the second.

Verify ./tools-test.sh fails with the resident's shadow is the size of their stance — shadow 86px, they stand 60px wide. Put it back and it passes. This bug survived four rounds of "the shadows are still wrong" before anybody measured it.
Debugging· an hour

13. Audit a sprite pack

a new script, anywhere

One of the twenty accessories in this pack is not a hat. acc/1.png is a small grey object drawn at rows 18–36 of its frame, which is above the hair — in game it floats over the character's head like a bucket. It was in every screenshot for weeks and nobody looked at it.

Write something that finds this class of problem: for every file in assets/char/, take a frame, get its bounding box, and report any accessory whose art finishes above the row where the head starts. Then do the same idea for furniture: which pieces have so much transparent padding that their depth would be wrong without base_offset()?

Verify Your script should find exactly one bad accessory out of twenty, and about forty-two furniture sprites with padding underneath. If it finds none, your alpha threshold is counting invisible pixels.
Behaviour· an hour

14. Give the cat a mind of its own

scripts/Pet.gd

The dog has four modes — follow, wander, sit, nap — and the cat only really uses two. Give the cat something the dog does not have: a favourite piece of furniture it returns to, or a habit of sitting in whichever room you are not in. The mode machine is a single match in _process; a new mode is a new branch that sets _target.

Verify Add a check to the walking group in the suite: put the cat somewhere, wait, and assert it ended up where its new rule says it should. The existing dog-follow check is the pattern to copy.
Graphics· an hour

15. Make a light mean something

scripts/Assets.gd → LIGHTS · scripts/RoomView.gd → tick_lights()

Right now every light is driven by one number: how dark the sky is. Make one of them react to the game instead — the stove glowing only while something is cooking, the television only when it is on, the bakery cases going dark when the shop shuts. tick_lights() already receives a clock; give it something else to read.

Verify Set the time to night in Settings, then trigger the state and watch the light change. Then consider what happens in a room you are not standing in — the lights are rebuilt per room, so a light for a room you have left does not exist.
Architecture· a weekend

16. Split the biggest file

scripts/Overlays.gd — 2,531 lines

The card framework is about 250 lines: curtain, panel, scroll, fit, pop-in. The other 2,280 are forty-three independent builders that share nothing but a habit of appending rows to _body. Pull them out by area — shops, story, pets, settings — leaving Overlays owning the frame and the dispatch.

The interesting question is what the builders need in order to live elsewhere. They currently reach for _body, _swap_body(), _title() and close() as though they were local. Passing a small object that offers exactly those four things is the difference between a split and a mess.

Verify ./tools-test.sh opens all forty-three cards and measures each one. If the split is clean, the number of passing checks does not change at all — which is the entire point of having them.

Part eight

Where it is weak

Nothing here is broken. These are the places the code would resist you if you came back to it cold in six months, in the order I would deal with them.

Overlays.gd — 2,531 lines, 43 card builders in one file

The obvious split

The card framework — curtain, panel, scroll, fit, pop-in — is about 250 lines and genuinely shared. The other 2,280 are independent builders. Splitting them by area (shops, story, pets, settings) would cost nothing structurally, since every builder does nothing but append rows to _body.

Game._sanitize() — 272 lines, the longest function here

Tedious for a reason, but it could be a table

Every field validated by hand. That behaviour is right and should not be weakened, but most of the lines are the same shape: a key, an expected type, a range or a set of valid values. A table plus one loop would express the same guarantees in a fraction of the space and make it obvious when a new state field has been added without validation — which is the actual risk in there today.

DecorCatalog.gd — 1,080 generated lines, checked in

Fine, but never hand-edit it

Machine-written from the web build's catalogue, and it says so at the top. The only real risk is somebody eventually editing it by hand and losing the change on the next regeneration.

The suite runs the whole game, and takes 36 seconds

Fast enough now; watch it

Most of that is settling time — waiting real milliseconds for rooms and cards to lay out. It is the price of testing what is drawn rather than what exists, and it is worth paying. If it creeps past a minute, split it: the data group needs no rendering at all and could run in under a second on its own.

Assets._base_cache — never cleared

Harmless, and worth knowing why

Art-base measurements are cached forever, keyed by id or by resource path plus scale. Since art files do not change while the game runs, that is correct. If you ever add hot-reloading of textures, this is the thing that will show you stale numbers.

Everything measured here was measured on 25 August 2026, on Godot 4.7.1, against the commit that adds tests/Suite.gd. Line counts come from grep -c "", function lengths from a parse of the script files, and every render figure from a windowed run that screenshotted itself. The three screens in Part two are unedited frames from that run, with markers drawn on afterwards.
